The Register of Architects

The Architects Register is the statutory register of architects in the United Kingdom, kept by the Architects Registration Board. Registration is required to use the title “architect”; it is not a licence to carry out architectural work, which is not reserved by law.

Assessment

Strengths

  • The board was established by Parliament in 1997 to regulate the architects’ profession, so this is a statutory register rather than a membership list.
  • The title “architect” is protected, which makes the register a real gate on who may use it and gives the search a definite legal meaning.
  • The search offers registration number as well as name and company, so a person can be identified without relying on name matching alone.

Limitations

  • It protects a title, not an activity. Registration is required to use the word “architect”, not to carry out architectural work.
  • It records registration status only. It is not a statement about competence, current insurance or disciplinary history.
  • No search was executed during verification, so result content and any limits were not observed and none is asserted.
  • It registers individual practitioners, not practices. A firm is not registered here, so the register does not establish anything about the business an architect works for.

Best for

  • Checking whether a person is entitled to call themselves an architect in the United Kingdom.
  • Confirming an architect’s registration number and recorded details before engaging them.

Not recommended for

  • Establishing that someone may lawfully design a building. Architectural work is not reserved by law, so an unregistered person may lawfully do it — they simply may not use the title.
  • Judging competence for a particular project. Registration records entitlement to the title, not skill, insurance or fitness for a commission.
