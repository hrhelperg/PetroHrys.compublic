How this works

What “newly observed” means

It means new to our monitoring, not necessarily newly published. A notice can enter the corpus because a source window moved, while the buyer published it weeks earlier. The publication date is shown on the opportunity itself.

Why a disappearance is not a cancellation

Sources use bounded windows and sometimes fail. A notice absent from the latest snapshot is reported as no longer observed, never as cancelled — and only when the source refreshed successfully with a complete window. Otherwise nothing is reported at all.

Deadline changes

A deadline is only described as extended or shortened when both values resolve to a real instant. Some sources publish a date with no time zone, which spans 26 hours; those changes are shown as “deadline changed” instead.

Supplier matching

Relevance is scored from the classification codes the buyer published, the geography and whether a route to act exists. It is a relevance score, never a probability of winning.

Limitations

Foreign supplier eligibility is never claimed here. No source publishes it per notice, and the eligibility recorded against a platform is a fact about the platform, not about any tender it carries.

The change ledger retains the most recent 0 events. It is observability for recent runs, not a historical archive.
