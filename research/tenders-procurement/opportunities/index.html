How this search works

Your words are matched against the tender title, the buyer name, classification codes and their published labels, and a short extract of the description. A title match counts for more than a description match, and an exact classification code counts for most. Put a phrase in quotation marks to require those words together.

Each result separates three things: what the source published, what we derived from it, and how the observation was made. Only the first is a fact from the buyer. A supplier-profile match is our calculation, not a statement by anyone procuring anything.

Relevance to your words and supplier-profile match are two different measurements and are never combined into one score. Choosing a supplier profile narrows which tenders are shown; it does not change how your words are ranked.

Tenders are classified by their source under CPV or UNSPSC. These are separate vocabularies and are not merged or mapped onto one another. The scheme is always shown next to the code, and codes are searchable exactly.

Declared values are shown in the currency the source published, and are never converted. Ordering by value therefore requires choosing one currency first.

A deadline is counted in days only when it resolves to an unambiguous moment. A date published without a time zone spans more than a day, so it is shown exactly as published and left out of the closing-within filters rather than being guessed into one.

Unknown is kept as unknown. A notice that does not mention electronic submission is not recorded as refusing it, and it is reachable through the “Not stated” option rather than being folded into “No”.

Whether tender documents can be downloaded is recorded for the platform as a whole, never for an individual notice, so there is no per-tender documents filter. Adding one would state something no source established.

Some sources need a real browser before their pages can be verified. That is a property of the source, and says nothing about whether a tender is genuine or worth bidding for.

Every source in this collection currently publishes from exactly one procurement platform, so choosing a source and choosing a platform would select the same records. Only one control is offered rather than two that appear to differ.

Some buyers publish many closely worded notices at once. Where wording, buyer, country and classification all agree, those opportunities are shown together under one result so a single procurement programme does not fill the page. Nothing is merged or removed, and every opportunity remains in the result count.

When your words also appear in a buyer's name, every notice from that buyer scores identically. Results that tie are ordered to vary the buyer and the wording rather than by an arbitrary internal identifier. Results with different relevance are never reordered, so nothing less relevant is ever promoted.

Filters and searches change this page's address so a search can be shared or reopened, but those addresses are deliberately kept out of search engines: they are states of one page, not thousands of separate pages.

Matched to supplier profiles

Each opportunity is scored against a supplier profile on category fit, geography, whether a route to act exists, how much time is left, and how well established the facts are. Only currently open opportunities are ranked.

This is an opportunity match, not a probability of winning. Nothing in the published data supports estimating competition, bidder numbers or chances, and none of those is calculated here.

Limitations

Foreign supplier eligibility is never claimed here. No pilot source publishes it per notice, and the eligibility recorded against a platform is a fact about the platform, not about any tender it carries.

Some sources publish a deadline with no time zone, and one publishes no deadline at all. Those are shown exactly as published and are never used to assert that something is still open.

Each source is ingested over a bounded, documented window rather than in full. Where the window could not be exhausted, the source table says so.

2613 ingested records are historical rather than current — awarded, closed, or withdrawn, including 8 cancelled. They are kept for the record and excluded from every recommendation.

Contract value appears only where the buyer published one. Nothing is estimated, converted between currencies, or summed across lots.
