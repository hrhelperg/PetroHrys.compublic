Corporations and Charities Filing System (CCFS)

Corporations and Charities Filing System (CCFS) is the state business-entity register operated by the Washington Secretary of State — Corporations and Charities Division. Search fields: Business entity and charity records via a Corporation Search / Business Search screen. Whether filed document images can be viewed free of charge is not stated on the official pages.

Assessment

Strengths

  • A single Corporations and Charities Filing System (CCFS) query reaches more than the entity register: it is also a charity register — the system is named, and operates as, the Corporations and Charities Filing System, with charities a register within it rather than an adjunct.
  • The Washington Secretary of State — Corporations and Charities Division is the statutory source for Washington, so an entry exists because a filing was made — not because anyone submitted a listing.

Limitations

  • No official page states whether filed document images can be viewed free of charge, so the cost of reading a filing cannot be established in advance.
  • Part of Corporations and Charities Filing System (CCFS) sits behind payment, an account or another restriction, so the free tier answers only part of a due-diligence question.
  • It does not prove good standing, does not prove tax compliance with the Washington Department of Revenue, does not prove that a business holds a Washington business licence (administered by the Department of Revenue's Business Licensing Service, not the Secretary of State), does not prove any professional licence, and does not prove solvency.

The PetroHrys Score is a first-party editorial assessment, not a third-party authority metric. It is the weighted sum of the ten factors above, divided by ten, so the number on this page is reproducible from the values shown. Each factor is scored from 0 to 10 by a human reviewer and weighted as shown. The definitions describe what was weighed, not a rubric: no threshold or formula maps a directory onto a value, and two reviewers could reach different numbers. The score is reproducible from the factors shown on every record, but it is an editorial judgement, not a measurement.
