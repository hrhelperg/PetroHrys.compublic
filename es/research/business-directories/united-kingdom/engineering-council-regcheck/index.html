RegCheck

RegCheck searches the Engineering Council Register, which records the individuals entitled to use the professional titles CEng, IEng, EngTech and ICTTech. Those titles are protected under the council’s Royal Charter; engineering practice itself is not reserved by law, so registration is not a licence to work as an engineer.

Valoración

Puntos fuertes

  • It is the register held by the body responsible for the UK engineering profession, rather than any one institution’s membership list.
  • The titles it records are protected under the council’s Royal Charter, so a confirmed entry has a definite meaning.
  • It includes people who registered in the past or hold inactive registrations, so a historical check is possible as well as a current one.

Limitaciones

  • It protects titles, not an activity, and the protection comes from a Royal Charter rather than from statute. Registration is not required to work as an engineer.
  • Search requires an exact match to the record held, so the council itself warns that an unexpected nil result does not establish that someone is unregistered.
  • Assessment is carried out by licensed professional engineering institutions rather than by the council directly, so an entry reflects an institution’s assessment as much as a central decision.
  • It registers individual practitioners, not employers. An engineering business is not registered here, so the register does not establish anything about the company an engineer works for.
  • No search was executed during verification, so result content, coverage and any rate limit were not observed and none is asserted.

Ideal para

  • Checking whether an individual currently holds a UK engineering professional title such as CEng, IEng or EngTech.
  • Verifying a title claim where the exact registered name is known.

No recomendado para

  • Establishing that someone may lawfully work as an engineer. Engineering practice is not generally reserved by law in the United Kingdom, and registration is not a licence to practise.
  • Concluding from a nil result that a person is unregistered. The council states that a search requires an exact match, so an unexpected result does not mean the person is not registered.

The PetroHrys Score is a first-party editorial assessment, not a third-party authority metric. It is the weighted sum of the ten factors above, divided by ten, so the number on this page is reproducible from the values shown. Each factor is scored from 0 to 10 by a human reviewer and weighted as shown. The definitions describe what was weighed, not a rubric: no threshold or formula maps a directory onto a value, and two reviewers could reach different numbers.
