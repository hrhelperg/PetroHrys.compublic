Directories for Enterprise Software

Where enterprise software vendors are listed in the verified dataset, and how enterprise buyers actually use those listings.

Introduction

Enterprise buyers use directories differently from small businesses. A listing is rarely the first touch; it is consulted during evaluation, often by someone building a shortlist for a committee. That changes what a good listing contains: depth, verification and comparability matter more than persuasion.

It also means the review platforms that verify their reviewers are worth disproportionately more than those that do not.

Evidence base

Evidence base: 259 of 295 verified directories in the PetroHrys dataset accept enterprise software. Every figure and table on this page is generated from those records at build time. Fields that were never established are shown as Unknown rather than filled in.

Decision framework

  • Platform marketplaces first if you integrate with a major platform. That is distribution into an existing customer base, not discovery.
  • Then a verified-review platform. Enterprise buyers discount unverified reviews heavily.
  • Then a comparison directory so you appear in category shortlists.
  • Confirm your statutory register entry is current. Procurement will check it.

Advantages recorded across these directories

  • Primary legal source for UK company data
  • Free public access to filings
  • It is the official publication venue for European procurement notices, so a notice there is the authoritative published text rather than a re-listing.
  • Access is free and anonymous, and the site says so in its own words rather than leaving a reader to discover it.
  • Coverage spans the European Union and the European Economic Area and beyond, with thousands of notices published every working day.
  • Free, no login required

Disadvantages recorded across these directories

  • Companies House states it does not verify the accuracy of filed information
  • It is a notice database, not a supplier register, a tender-submission system or a funding portal, and it should not be used as any of those.
  • A published notice records what a buyer announced. It is not a record of who won, unless a separate award notice was also published.
  • The site cautions that only electronically signed notices published in the Supplement to the Official Journal are authentic.
  • Publication establishes nothing about a supplier’s eligibility or standing. A notice records that a buyer announced a contract, not that any bidder qualifies for it.
  • Limited to publicly disclosable ABR fields

Recommendations

Prioritise directories that verify. A platform that confirms reviewers were recent users is more useful in a procurement conversation than one with ten times the review volume and no verification.

When not to use this

  • Vendors with no integration story and no reference customers yet. A thin enterprise listing invites unfavourable comparison.
  • Companies unwilling to sustain review collection, since an abandoned profile is read as a signal.

Frequently asked questions

Do enterprise marketplaces charge to list?

Most in this dataset do not publish listing pricing, so their submission model is recorded as Unknown. Several take a revenue share, which is a separate question from a listing fee.
